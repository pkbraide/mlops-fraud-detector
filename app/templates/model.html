What these models are, and are not

Every number on this page is read live from GET /metrics, which in turn reads the evaluation results stored inside each model artifact at training time. Nothing here is typed into a template.

Limitations — read this first

The v2 model scores near-perfectly because card_transdata is synthetic and its fraud label is close to a deterministic function of two features. Real fraud runs at roughly 0.1 % prevalence against adversaries who adapt. The v3 model, trained on real anonymised transactions at 0.172 % prevalence, is the honest benchmark — and its lower scores are the realistic ones. These results demonstrate the pipeline, not detection capability.

What these features cannot catch

  • Velocity and sequence. Both models see one transaction at a time. Ten small purchases in five minutes, or a €1 test charge before a large one, are invisible.
  • Merchant and category. v2 has a single "repeat retailer" bit; v3's PCA components hide merchant identity entirely. A first purchase at a pharmacy and a first purchase at a crypto exchange look alike.
  • Account and device context. New shipping address, new device fingerprint, recent password reset, login geography, account age — none of it is a feature.
  • Network effects. A merchant or IP already tied to other confirmed fraud carries no signal here.
  • Fraud that looks like the cardholder. Account takeover with the correct PIN, at home, for a usual amount is undetectable by construction with either feature set.
  • Interpretability (v3). V1–V28 are anonymised principal components. A high score cannot be explained to a cardholder or an analyst in business terms.

Why PR-AUC, not accuracy

At 0.172 % prevalence a model that never flags anything is 99.83 % accurate. Accuracy is dominated by the easy negatives and says nothing about the class that matters. ROC-AUC is better but still rewards ranking a fraud above the enormous pool of trivial legitimate rows. Precision–recall curves, and their area (average precision), are computed only from how many frauds are retrieved and how clean the retrieved set is — the two quantities that map directly onto analyst workload and prevented loss. That is why the tables below lead with fraud-class precision, recall, F1 and PR-AUC, and why accuracy appears nowhere as a headline.

Why the rule-based fallback exists

If an artifact is missing, corrupt, or built for a different feature order, its route does not crash and does not silently score shuffled columns. It switches to a fixed, documented rule, reports model_version: "mock_rule", and returns confidence: null rather than a made-up probability. The badge in the page header turns amber. This keeps the API contract, the tests and the deployment green on a fresh clone while making the degraded state impossible to miss.
